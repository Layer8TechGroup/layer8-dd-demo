DD Demo Guide

How to present the Layer8 DD Assessment in a buyer or broker meeting

1. How to Demo the DD Portfolio in a Buyer Meeting

The goal of every buyer meeting is to get the buyer to recognize that they are about to make one of the largest financial decisions of their life without the tools to do it properly. The demo portfolio makes that visible.

0:00
Open with the question
“Have you seen a deal fall apart after LOI? What happened?” Let them answer. Most buyers have a story. That story is your opening.
3:00
Show the portfolio index
Open index.html. Point to the risk score column. “These 12 companies were all assessed as sellers and scored 3.5–8.2 on exit readiness. Here’s what buyers would find.”
6:00
Open a contrasting pair
Show Meridian (3.3/10, PROCEED) vs. Pinnacle (7.0/10, PROCEED WITH CAUTION). Use the ER demo link at demo.layer8techgroup.com to show the same company from the seller side. The contrast is immediate and visceral.
12:00
Walk through one full report
Use Garrison (5.3, PROCEED WITH STRUCTURE). It has relatable risk for an SBA buyer — owner dependency, book portability — without being so high-risk it feels hypothetical.
20:00
Deal structure page
Show the deal structure recommendations section. Ask: “If you were buying Garrison and we told you there’s a 1.7-point gap between what the seller told you and what we found, how much is that worth to you?”
25:00
Close on intake
Ask: “Do you have a deal in diligence right now? Let’s run Stage 1 intake before you leave. 15 minutes.” Pull up the intake form.

2. Key Talking Points by Risk Level

LOW RISK (1–3)

“This is what a clean deal looks like. You still need diligence — but at this risk level, diligence confirms what you already believe. You’re looking for surprises, not disasters.”

Use Meridian as the example.

MODERATE (4–5)

“This is the most dangerous zone for buyers — risky enough to hurt you, but not risky enough to make you walk. This is where deals get restructured post-LOI.”

Use Garrison or Peachtree as examples.

HIGH RISK (6–7)

“At this level the report is basically your negotiating brief. Every finding is a lever. You need an escrow, an earnout, and a walk condition in your back pocket.”

Use Pinnacle as the example. Pull up the deal structure page.

5. Handling Pricing Questions

“How much does it cost?” “The Stage 1 intake and Stage 2 data guide are free. If you want Layer8 to execute the full Stage 3 assessment, that starts at $3,500 for a straightforward deal. For context: what does your attorney charge per hour? A full DD assessment is typically a fraction of what you’d spend on legal, and it tells your attorney exactly where to look.”
“Can’t I just do this myself?” “Absolutely. Here’s what Stage 2 gives you for free — a complete document checklist and question bank specific to your target company and buyer type. Most buyers use that and then come back when they find something unexpected. That’s fine.”
“Is this the same as a QofE?” “No. A QofE is financial only. We cover eight domains including operational, legal, technology, customer, and management risk. We also tell you what deal structure to use based on what we find, and give you a 180-day integration plan. The QofE firm gives you an adjusted EBITDA. We give you a playbook.”
“The seller already gave me a clean CIM.” “The CIM is a marketing document. It was written to make you buy. Our report is written from the other direction — to tell you what the CIM doesn’t. Open Pinnacle’s report and count how many red flags the CIM would never mention.”
